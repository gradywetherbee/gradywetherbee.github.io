# On pairing sinks with faucets: a study in usability

dear sink and faucet designers, 

i have a bone to pick with you.



they say good design is design you don't notice

i know for sure that bad design is design you can't help but notice

design that runs counter to your goals

take sinks



often when washing my hands in businesses, i find that the design of the sink is noticeably broken because my hands are forced into bumping the bottom or back of the sink because there isn't enough clearance between where the water comes out and the bottom/back of the sink

i have big hands.. but im no NBA player

is this a huge problem?

not really, no

    but it's so painfully easy to avoid in the first place that it bugs me every time



let's say the 99th percentile hand length in the US is 8 inches 

and let's say the 99th percentile hand width is 6 inches. this could also be called "height" when one's hands are held palm to palm in typical hand-washing orientation

and let's say we want the water from a faucet to drop roughly into the middle or lower, lengthwise, of your hands when washing them, meaning somewhere around the top of your palm rather than high up on your fingers



unless you are designing a sink for an extremely constrained space like an airplane or an RV here's the solution:

make sure the back-to-front gap between the back of the sink drainage area and where the water comes out of the faucet, as seen from above, is at least 5 inches

    4 inches being half of a large hand, plus an additional inch of space so that your hands never touch the back of the sink

then make sure the vertical gap between the bottom of the sink basin and where the water comes out of the faucet is at least 7 inches

    6 inches being the thumb-to-pinky width/height of a large hand, plus an additional inch of space so that your hands never touch the bottom of the sink while washing them
